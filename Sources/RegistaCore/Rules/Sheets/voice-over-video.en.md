# Voice-over video

No one on camera: the user's voice over footage, stills and graphics.

- Show each piece when the voice names it, not before.
- The screen never repeats the spoken sentence word for word: titles say what the voice does not.
- Each picture stays long enough for its line: at least the length of the narration over it plus a little air.
- Change the picture every few seconds, without a mechanical rhythm.
- End on the one idea in one line; the logo, when there is one, comes after the last word.
- The person's face is not on screen unless they ask for it.
- Every recording the person hands over goes, moving, under the line that tells that step.
- Frame 0 is the person's own opening shot, never moved or replaced, with at most two still titles: the subject and the angle.
- A landscape recording in a vertical frame is shown whole, edge to edge, never cut at the sides; the empty bands show the same picture behind it, blurred and darker.
- The turn is the picture that changes most: its stage, its size or its movement breaks with what came before.
- A sound effect only on something that happens on screen; two actions never share one effect.
